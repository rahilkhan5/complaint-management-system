# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three kinds of people use this app, each in a different setting:

- **Residents** of a housing community (inspired by Bahria Town Karachi). They open the app on their phone, often outdoors or at home, usually annoyed because something is broken: no water, a power fault, a blocked drain. They want to report it in under a minute and later check whether anyone is working on it.
- **Support agents** at the complaint center. They sit at an office desktop for a full shift, work through a queue of assigned complaints, update statuses, and leave notes for residents.
- **Admins** (complaint center supervisors). On desktop, they watch the whole queue, assign complaints to agents, and manage staff accounts.

A secondary audience: recruiters and interviewers reviewing Rahil Khan's portfolio. They spend two or three minutes clicking through with demo accounts and judge whether the work looks like a real product.

## Product Purpose

Replace phone calls and paper registers with one place where every complaint is recorded, assigned, tracked through a clear status flow (Open, In Progress, Resolved, Closed), and never lost. Success means a resident always knows the current status of their complaint, an agent always knows what to work on next, and an admin can see the backlog at a glance.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, clear, calm. The interface should feel like a well-run service desk: official without being cold, the way a good banking app reassures you that your request was received. Copy is plain and direct, in simple English that a non-native reader understands instantly. No jokes in the UI, no exclamation marks, no hype.

## Anti-references

- Old government portals: dense grey tables, tiny fonts, everything the same weight, no hierarchy.
- Cartoonish or over-colored apps: emoji in the UI, bright colors on every element, playful illustrations.
- Generic admin templates where every page is the same row of four stat cards plus a chart.

## Design Principles

1. **Status is the product.** Every screen answers "where does this complaint stand?" before anything else. Status is always visible, color-coded and labeled in words, never color alone.
2. **One obvious next action.** Each role lands on the screen that shows what to do now: residents see their complaints and a clear way to file a new one, agents see their queue, admins see what is unassigned.
3. **Phone first for residents, desk first for staff.** The resident flow must be effortless on a small screen; staff screens can be denser because they are used all day on desktop.
4. **Calm under frustration.** People arrive upset. Clear confirmation, predictable layout and honest error messages lower the temperature.
5. **Explainable in an interview.** Simple, conventional patterns and code a junior developer can walk through line by line.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text contrast at least 4.5:1, status never conveyed by color alone, full keyboard navigation with visible focus, form fields with real labels and clear error messages, touch targets at least 44px on mobile, and reduced motion respected.
